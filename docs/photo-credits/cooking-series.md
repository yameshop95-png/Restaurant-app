# Photo credits — cooking-series blog covers

All three photos are from Pexels and are used under the [Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/): free for commercial use; attribution is not required, but photographer and source are recorded here anyway. None are AI-generated. Unsplash was searched as well; these three Pexels photos were the closest honest matches.

Each file was resized to a maximum width of 1600 px and saved as WebP at quality 80. Content was not cropped, composited or otherwise altered.

The cover files live in `public/site/photos/` and each post's `cover` field is `/site/photos/<filename>.webp`. That is the same convention used by existing posts in `src/site/data/posts.js` (for example `cover: '/site/photos/banh-cuon.webp'`).

## hanh-phi.webp

- **Source page:** https://www.pexels.com/photo/food-on-plate-near-bowl-with-sauce-and-chopsticks-2134033/
- **Photographer:** Quang Nguyen Vinh ([Pexels profile](https://www.pexels.com/@quang-nguyen-vinh-222549/))
- **Licence:** Pexels License
- **Original:** 4500 × 3000 JPEG, landscape 3:2, taken 30 January 2015
- **Match:** Honest, but not a standalone bowl of shallots on paper towel. No clear photo of freshly fried shallots draining on their own was found on Pexels or Unsplash. This photo does clearly show golden crispy fried shallots (hành phi) piled on Vietnamese steamed rice rolls, with sliced pork roll, herbs and a bowl of dipping sauce. That is the way hành phi is served, and it is not a different dish being passed off as fried shallots.
- **Alt text:** Golden crispy fried shallots scattered over Vietnamese steamed rice rolls, served with sliced pork roll, herbs and a small bowl of dipping sauce.
